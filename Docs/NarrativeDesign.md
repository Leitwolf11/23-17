# 23.17 mystery and horror design

23.17 is a first-person investigative horror anthology. The detective can open any case file. Each investigation ends with an evidence-backed account of the central mystery. Sounds and sightings at the edge of the investigation can remain unexplained.

## The Apartment

Alex falls in an apartment stairwell during a rainy November night. The caretaker claims to recognize Alex at 23:17 on a windowless landing, during a 23:11–23:22 outage. The synchronized dispatch records a bell at 23:14, four minutes ahead of the camera. The correction places Alex's arrival at 23:18. A stair recorder and contact sensor establish that the unsafe railing failed at 23:19 while Alex was alone. The service exit opened at 23:21, and the sensor provides no identity. A signed revision links the false repair entry at 23:34 to the building manager, Mara Voss.

The staircase gradually loses light, and the rain recedes as the player moves away from the street windows. When the detective stops on the stairs, another set of footsteps continues briefly. The player can back down into the lobby.

## Night Shift

Flood relocation files are missing from a public archive. The catalogue still shows box A-41 in public storage. The shelf ledger, transfer slip, guard's statement and intake record place the box on a route through lift B into restricted conservation. Directive D-7 attributes the transfer and the misleading catalogue entry to director Eva Holm.

A disconnected extension rings among the stacks. Another telephone rings at the intake desk when the detective returns. Neither event affects the documented route.

## The Last Screening

Projectionist René Vogel is missing after the last screening. A screening copy, reel leader and projection diary establish that booth supervisor Ada Weiss moved the balcony insert before the closing title. The staff exit register and the taxi driver's dispatch log establish that René left alive for the night train station.

The projector starts in the dark. A seat appears occupied on the projected image. The image can be seen from the aisle without blocking the exit.

## Investigation and accessibility

Read original evidence in the journal, pick two records to compare, challenge each witness statement with a collected source, order four events and draft a report. A file can stay incomplete while facts are missing, require revision when the timeline or findings conflict with the records, or close when the evidence supports the report. Each file saves its own progress. Returning starts from a safe entrance or a later checkpoint earned by finding an essential record.

Critical narrative evidence is available in writing. Environmental audio, sudden effects and sound captions have independent controls. Pausing or returning to the case selector leaves the player free to resume later.

## Release targets

- Expand the playable cases to 45–60 minutes of exploration, inference and testimony per case, measured in first-time playtests.
- Establish the art quality bar for the Apartment with authored glTF spaces, textured materials and skinned, animated witnesses. Carry that production standard into the archive and cinema.
- Target **PEGI 16** with the **Horror** descriptor through sustained dread. Convey the fall through its aftermath and the records. The game is awaiting classification. [PEGI Horror descriptor](https://pegi.info/fear)
- Validate US English, German and French on Linux and Windows with both graphics APIs where supported. The reference machine targets p99 frame time at or below 20 ms at 1280×720 High. Record 1366×768 measurements alongside the acceptance runs.
